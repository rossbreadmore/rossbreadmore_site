Weekend route notes — Felpham & Bognor Regis

The Felpham Close Loop

A tight circuit around Felpham and Bognor Regis for drawing and photographing architecture and coastline — nothing further than a 2–3 mile ride, grouped by direction from base, with somewhere to eat nearby.

Base
6 Havelock Close, Felpham, PO22 7AX

ON FOOT

Felpham village

under 1 mi

Worth doing first, in low morning or evening light, before the seafront gets busy.

Blake's Cottage0.5 mi

Thatched, flint-and-brick 18th-century cottage where William Blake lived 1800–1803 — the archetypal English cottage silhouette (steep thatch, small-paned windows), quiet lane, good for a slow architectural study.

FoodThe Southdowns, a short walk away on Limmer Lane — or save eating for the seafront, ten minutes further on.
St Mary's Church, Felpham0.4 mi

The old parish church at the heart of the village — flint walls, a modest tower, and a churchyard worth a slower pencil study before the ride properly starts.

FoodSame as Blake's Cottage above — they're a minute apart.
SW

Bognor Regis seafront

1.5–2 mi

Flat, traffic-free promenade the whole way from Felpham.

The Pier & Esplanade1.7 mi

What remains of the Victorian pier against open sea, plus the sweep of the Esplanade itself — good subject range from a wide seascape down to individual railings and beach-hut colour.

FoodThe Lobster Pot or The Boat House, both on the prom near the pier.
Royal Norfolk Hotel & Waterloo Square1.8 mi

The Royal Norfolk's Regency front faces the sea from the Esplanade, and the terraces stepping back from Waterloo Square behind it are the best run of period seafront architecture in Bognor — worth a study of the ironwork balconies and bay windows.

FoodThe Royal Norfolk's own bar, or the cafés dotted along the square.
W

Bognor town & Hotham Park

2–2.5 mi

A short ride inland from the seafront, through the town centre.

Hotham Park & Hotham Park House2.3 mi

A white Regency mansion (1792) set in its own parkland, with a walled garden, miniature railway and mature trees around it — a change of pace from seafront and cottage subjects, with a proper set-back facade to draw.

FoodHotham Park Café, inside the park itself.
Picturedrome & the town centre streets2 mi

The Picturedrome is a listed early-1900s cinema front, and London Road and the streets around it carry a mix of Victorian and Edwardian shopfronts — good for quick urban sketching between errands, less polished than the seafront.

FoodPlenty of cafés and bakeries along London Road and the pedestrianised centre.
W

Aldwick

~2.7 mi

Continue straight along the promenade past the pier.

Aldwick Beach & the beach huts2.7 mi

Rows of painted beach huts along the prom, quieter and less built-up than central Bognor — good repeating-colour subject matter, and a contrast to the grander Waterloo Square terraces closer to home.

FoodAldwick Beach Café or West Park Café, both right on the front.
E

Middleton-on-Sea & Elmer

2–3 mi

The other direction along the coast from Felpham — quieter lanes than the Bognor side.

St Nicholas Church, Middleton-on-Sea2 mi

A small flint parish church a short way inland from the current shoreline (the medieval village and its original church were lost to coastal erosion) — modest, quiet, and a good contrast to Felpham's church closer to home.

FoodThe Beresford on Elmer Road, or The Elmer, both dining pubs nearby.
Elmer Sands rock groynes3 mi

Rows of large sculptural rock groynes sunk into the beach as sea defences — strong graphic shapes at low tide, especially side-lit, and a change of pace from building studies. This is the far edge of the loop.

FoodThe Beresford and The Elmer (above) are the nearest options, a short ride back toward Middleton.

Notes before you set off

  • Distances are approximate one-way road distances from Havelock Close — the whole loop above, doorstep to doorstep, is comfortably under 10 miles for the day if you string several clusters together.
  • Quick day plan: Felpham village on foot first thing, then seafront and town by bike, saving Aldwick or Middleton/Elmer for whichever has the better light that afternoon.
  • Pace it for drawing, not distance — at this range you can easily reach every spot on this list in a weekend, so the limiting factor is time spent actually sitting and drawing at each one, not the cycling between them.
  • Best light: the seafront and Aldwick/Elmer beach huts favour low, raking morning or evening light; Hotham Park and the town streets work fine at any time of day.
